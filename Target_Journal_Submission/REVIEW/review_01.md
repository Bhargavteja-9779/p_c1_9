# Review 01: R1-novelty

**Recommendation:** major_revision

**Summary:** The genuinely novel core is the systematic, multi-benchmark measurement that standardization choice barely moves aggregate metrics yet substantially shifts individual predictions in a strongly representation-dependent way (C1/C2) on the chemical-graph axis; this is a real, publishable gap and the string-vs-graph distinction from Kikuchi is correct at the axis level. However, the paper materially overstates its methodological and conceptual novelty: SORB is the well-established train-augmentation + test-time-averaging (TTA-as-ensemble) paradigm transplanted to a new augmentation axis, and the closest method prior art (Bjerrum 2017; Zhang 2018; Kimber/maxsmi 2021) is entirely uncited; and several conceptual contributions (the confounder framing, the semi-synthetic shortcut experiment, and the reporting protocol) substantially overlap Kikuchi 2025's already-published "notation-level confounding," which the paper credits only as a narrow orthogonal string axis. The foundational curation canon (Fourches/Tropsha) is also missing. Novelty is salvageable by honest repositioning plus added citations; no new experiment is strictly required.

## Findings

### [MAJOR] Methodological novelty / closest prior art uncited (SORB = TTA-as-ensemble on a new axis)
- **Issue:** SORB is defined (Abstract L26-33; Sec 4.2 L151-170; contribution C3 L78-82) as 'augment training over, and average inference across' each molecule's orbit, plus an optional consistency-regularized variant. This is exactly the established data-augmentation + test-time-augmentation-as-ensemble paradigm for molecular property prediction: Bjerrum 2017 (SMILES enumeration as train- and test-time augmentation), Zhang et al. 2018 (inference-time SMILES averaging explicitly framed as ensemble consensus), and Kimber et al. 2021 (maxsmi) which formalizes test-time augmentation as ensembling and even uses the across-augmentation prediction variance as a confidence/uncertainty signal (structurally identical to this paper's orbit prediction SD invariance metric). The consistency-regularized neural variant is standard consistency regularization. None of these are cited anywhere (references.bib contains no Bjerrum/Zhang/Kimber/maxsmi). The Related Work paragraph on augmentation (L106-109) cites only Kikuchi and tautaug2021 and calls SORB 'analogous in spirit', which understates that the method mechanism itself is prior art.
- **Evidence:** WebSearch confirmed the TTA-as-ensemble lineage is the dominant design in molecular property prediction (train with augmentation, average equivalent representations at inference, treat spread as confidence). The paper presents SORB as a new 'representation-agnostic method/scheme' without acknowledging the mechanism is established; by ACS/JCIM standards, presenting a known recipe applied to a new input axis as a novel method, while omitting the nearest method citations, is a prior-art failure. Note the axis distinction actually helps the paper (canonical fingerprints are notation-invariant, so SMILES-TTA is inapplicable to the fingerprint/descriptor models here) — but that argument is never made because the lineage is never acknowledged.
- **Fix:** Cite Bjerrum 2017, Zhang 2018, and Kimber/maxsmi 2021, and add a subsection in Related Work and Sec 4 that explicitly frames SORB as the augmentation+test-time-averaging-ensemble paradigm applied to the graph-standardization orbit. Reframe the C3 contribution from 'a new method' to 'transferring the augmentation-ensemble paradigm to the previously-unstudied graph-standardization axis, where string-level SMILES-TTA is inapplicable because graph featurizations are notation-invariant' — this is a legitimate and defensible novelty claim. Also note that using orbit SD as a reliability metric parallels maxsmi's use of TTA variance as confidence, and credit it.
- **Requires new experiment:** False

### [MAJOR] Conceptual novelty / under-crediting Kikuchi 2025
- **Issue:** The paper credits Kikuchi 2025 only as a narrow 'pure notation axis' that is 'orthogonal' and affects only sequence models (Intro L57-61; Related Work L106-109), then presents as fresh: (a) the 'silent confounder' framing (Abstract L26; title of Sec 6.4), (b) the semi-synthetic 'standardization-as-shortcut' experiment (Sec 7.3 L299-307, contribution C4), and (c) the reporting-protocol recommendation (C5; Discussion L334-337). But Kikuchi's actual paper introduces 'notation-level confounding' precisely as equivalent-representation variants that 'spuriously correlate with class labels and inflate predictive performance', reports that notation inconsistency 'minimally affects property prediction' (parallel to this paper's RQ1 aggregate-insensitivity 'reassuring' finding, Sec 6.2), audits how inconsistencies appear in benchmarks, and recommends 'explicit reporting of preprocessing tools and settings'. So the shortcut/confounder framing and the reporting recommendation are the graph-axis analogues of already-published contributions, not new concepts.
- **Evidence:** WebSearch of arXiv:2505.07139 (v3/v4) confirms Kikuchi's confounding = spurious correlation of notation with labels inflating performance, the minimal-property-prediction-effect finding, and the preprocessing-reporting recommendation. The manuscript's Section 7.3 shortcut design (standardization state made predictive of label; naive model exploits it) is conceptually the same phenomenon on the graph axis. Presenting these as novel overstates the conceptual contribution.
- **Fix:** Rewrite the Kikuchi positioning to acknowledge the overlap explicitly: state that the confounder framing, the shortcut-exploitation phenomenon, and the preprocessing-reporting recommendation were introduced by Kikuchi for the SMILES-string axis, and that this work's novelty is (i) the chemical-graph axis that affects all featurizations (not just language models), (ii) the systematic multi-benchmark, representation-dependent per-prediction quantification, and (iii) a concrete robust-learning remedy and orbit formalization. Temper 'silent confounder' as new terminology.
- **Requires new experiment:** False

### [MODERATE] Missing foundational prior art (curation canon and assay-heterogeneity)
- **Issue:** The motivation (Intro L36-66; Related Work L90-96) cites QSAR-ready/PubChem/curation-review works but omits the foundational curation canon that directly established that structure standardization/curation errors propagate into QSAR model performance: Fourches, Muratov, Tropsha 'Trust, But Verify: On the Importance of Chemical Structure Curation in Cheminformatics and QSAR Modeling Research' (JCIM 2010) and 'Trust, but Verify II' (JCIM 2016). The journal-selection notes also reference assay-heterogeneity work ('AssayMatch', Landrum noise, 'Three-Level Noise Floor'); only landrum2024/crusius2024 are cited. These are directly on-point for 'does standardization matter for models' and their omission weakens the literature-review rigor expected at JCIM.
- **Evidence:** WebSearch surfaced the Fourches/Tropsha 'Trust, But Verify' papers as the canonical references for structure-curation impact on QSAR, and a 2026 curation review reiterating that curation materially affects performance and reproducibility. A JCIM reviewer will expect these foundational works to be engaged, especially since the paper's thesis is standardization-affects-models.
- **Fix:** Add and distinguish Fourches/Muratov/Tropsha (2010, 2016) and the relevant assay-heterogeneity papers: credit them for establishing that curation/standardization errors affect QSAR, then sharpen the gap as the per-prediction, representation-dependent, multi-benchmark invariance measurement plus the remedy, which those recommendation/error-propagation works do not provide.
- **Requires new experiment:** False

### [MODERATE] 'Silent confounder / per-prediction invariance' framing is partly a relabeling
- **Issue:** The central framing (Abstract; Sec 6.4; Problem Formulation Sec 3) rests on two ideas that are not themselves new: (i) aggregate metrics hide per-sample variability, and (ii) prediction spread under equivalent-input augmentation is a reliability/uncertainty signal (already used by Kimber/maxsmi 2021 as a confidence measure). The 'standardization orbit' is a relabeling of an 'augmentation set', and 'standardization invariance' is the molecular-property instance of the broad augmentation-invariance literature. The genuinely novel claim is narrower: the magnitude and strong representation-dependence of this spread on the graph-standardization axis, measured across 55 benchmarks.
- **Evidence:** The problem formulation (L118-135) defines standardization sensitivity s(x)=SD over the orbit, which is the augmentation-variance quantity used elsewhere as uncertainty; wrapping it in new terminology risks a reviewer reading the conceptual contribution as presentation rather than substance.
- **Fix:** State the precise novel claim explicitly (representation-dependent magnitude of per-prediction standardization sensitivity across many benchmarks, and that aggregate metrics conceal it) and drop or qualify the implication that the confounder/invariance framing is itself novel. Relate s(x) to the known augmentation-variance-as-uncertainty literature.
- **Requires new experiment:** False

### [MINOR] Over-claiming 'first / not quantified at scale'
- **Issue:** Claims such as 'to our knowledge, has not been quantified at scale' (Intro L55), 'What is missing is a systematic, multi-dataset measurement...' (L64-66), and 'First multi-dataset... quantification' (research plan C1) are defensible for the diagnosis but are stated broadly given the adjacent TTA/notation/curation literature now shown to exist.
- **Evidence:** No multi-benchmark per-prediction standardization-invariance study surfaced, so the core diagnosis gap is real; but the surrounding claims should be scoped to the exact novel axis to avoid an easy reviewer rebuttal that 'standardization affects models' is long known (Fourches/Tropsha) and that equivalent-representation confounding is published (Kikuchi).
- **Fix:** Scope the novelty claims tightly: 'first systematic, multi-benchmark, representation-dependent measurement of per-prediction sensitivity to the chemical-graph standardization choice, together with a robust-learning remedy.' Avoid unqualified 'first/not quantified' phrasing.
- **Requires new experiment:** False
