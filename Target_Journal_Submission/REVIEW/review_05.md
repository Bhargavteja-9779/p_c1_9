# Review 05: R5-dataset (Dataset Validity & Generalization)

**Recommendation:** major_revision

**Summary:** The core empirical observation — that standardization choices barely move aggregate metrics but substantially move individual predictions — is plausible and important, and the leakage/duplicate auditing is more thorough than most QSAR papers. However, the paper's central assumption (that the modeled property is exactly standardization-invariant for every endpoint used) is simply false for a meaningful fraction of the 55 benchmarks: logD7.4 lipophilicity is by definition protonation-dependent, FreeSolv hydration free energy is tautomer/protomer-specific, and several ADMET permeability/PK endpoints depend on the neutral-species fraction. For those endpoints part of the measured "orbit prediction SD" is genuine chemical signal, not a spurious invariance violation, so the headline magnitudes are inflated and SORB's "enforced invariance" may average away real information. Two further dataset-validity problems — a duplicated dataset (lipophilicity counted twice, breaking the independence assumption of the multi-dataset tests) and a pre-registered deduplication step that was never implemented (851 conflicting-label duplicate groups and 82 residual split leaks remain) — plus an external-validity gap (the artificially enumerated orbit does not estimate realistic between-toolkit disagreement; method/shortcut results are on 49 of 55 datasets while Section 8 claims 55) warrant major revision.

## Findings

### [MAJOR] Problem formulation / endpoint validity (Section 3, 11-ii; datasets.py TDC_REG, _MOLNET)
- **Issue:** The entire thesis rests on the property being exactly invariant across the standardization orbit, and Limitation (ii) claims this 'holds for the physicochemical and ADMET endpoints used' and that endpoints whose measured species is a specific tautomer/protonation state are 'excluded'. They are not excluded. (a) tdc:lipophilicity_astrazeneca and molnet:lipophilicity are AstraZeneca logD7.4 — the distribution coefficient at pH 7.4 is BY DEFINITION ionization-dependent, yet the orbit explicitly includes a 'pH 7.4 protonation' member and a 'neutralized' member (standardize.py op_protonate_ph74 / op_neutralize; OrbitConfig include_ph74/include_neutral). (b) molnet:freesolv is hydration free energy, a physical quantity defined for a specific neutral tautomer; different tautomers have genuinely different dG_hyd, yet the orbit enumerates up to 4 tautomers. (c) Passive-permeability/absorption endpoints (caco2_wang, hia_hou, pgp_broccatelli, bbb_martins) and ionization-sensitive PK endpoints (ppbr_az, vdss_lombardo, clearance_*, half_life) depend on the neutral-species fraction and the ionized form. For all of these, some of the orbit prediction spread reflects real chemical sensitivity, so the claim 'every unit of this spread is spurious' (Section 6.4) is wrong and the headline 0.10x-0.42x label-SD numbers are upper bounds that conflate spurious sensitivity with legitimate signal. Worse, for these endpoints SORB test-time orbit averaging would average predictions over chemically distinct species and could destroy real information rather than enforce a true symmetry.
- **Evidence:** logD is operationally defined as the pH-dependent octanol/water distribution coefficient; the neutral and pH-7.4 microspecies have different partition behavior, which is exactly the signal logD captures. FreeSolv ΔG_hyd is tautomer-specific. The orbit (standardize.py standardization_orbit) deliberately injects protonation and tautomer variants for every molecule regardless of endpoint. RQ5_invariance shows the largest spread exactly where descriptors/fingerprints are most protonation/tautomer-sensitive (linear+ecfp 0.42x), so the inflation is non-negligible.
- **Fix:** Either (i) restrict the invariance-violation headline to endpoints where invariance is genuinely defensible (e.g., single-affinity potency pKi/pEC50) and move logD7.4, FreeSolv, and permeability/PK endpoints to a clearly labeled 'endpoints with partial physical dependence' group reported separately, or (ii) decompose the orbit and report spread from invariance-safe axes (aromaticity model, stereo on achiral/invariant centers, salt) separately from chemically-meaningful axes (protonation, tautomer) for these endpoints. Re-slice the existing per-dataset per-axis results (RQ3 ablation + RQ5) rather than claiming uniform invariance. Delete the false blanket statement in Limitation (ii).
- **Requires new experiment:** False

### [MAJOR] Dataset count and multi-dataset statistics (Table 1; datasets.py; leakage_audit.csv)
- **Issue:** tdc:lipophilicity_astrazeneca and molnet:lipophilicity are the SAME AstraZeneca ChEMBL logD7.4 benchmark (both N=4200). The leakage audit confirms near-identical fingerprints (both 82 dup groups, 180 dup mols, 14 label disagreements, 98 std-collapse pairs; train/test NN 0.0143 vs 0.0155). This means (a) the '55 datasets' headline double-counts — there are 54 distinct datasets; and (b) every across-dataset statistic that treats datasets as independent units (Friedman/Nemenyi, pooled Wilcoxon over datasets, bootstrap CIs 'for headline differences across datasets', frac_top_model_changes, RQ4 pooled n) includes two perfectly correlated copies, violating the independence assumption and inflating effective n.
- **Evidence:** Table 1 lists both rows at N=4200; leakage_audit.csv rows for the two are statistically identical. The pooled regression n for RQ4 (n=160 = 40 datasets x 4) and the Friedman analysis in Section 8 treat these as separate exchangeable units.
- **Fix:** Drop one copy (or merge and keep a single logD7.4 benchmark), correct the dataset count throughout (abstract, Section 5, Section 12), and recompute all across-dataset tests and CIs without the duplicate. This is re-analysis of existing per-dataset numbers.
- **Requires new experiment:** False

### [MAJOR] Lower-bound caveat and real-world relevance of the measured effect (Section 6.2, 11-i; standardize.py orbit)
- **Issue:** The paper frames the phenomenon as a reproducibility failure between two labs that standardize the same benchmark differently, and honestly notes the benchmarks are pre-standardized so the effect is a 'lower bound'. But the per-prediction violation is actually measured against an ARTIFICIALLY ENUMERATED orbit (RDKit tautomer enumeration + Dimorphite re-protonation + MDL aromaticity re-perception) applied to already-canonicalized structures. This is neither a clean lower bound (the orbit re-introduces variation, including chemically marginal tautomers, that no single real pipeline would emit) nor an estimate of the actually-claimed scenario (disagreement between two real end-to-end toolkits). The quantity practitioners care about — 'if lab A uses RDKit and lab B uses ChemAxon/OpenBabel, how much do predictions disagree?' — is never measured. RQ3 shows only tautomer has a nonzero aggregate ablation delta (0.006; +ph74/+mdlarom/+stripstereo/-neutralize all 0.0), suggesting much of the engineered orbit spread comes from one axis and from enumeration artifacts rather than realistic cross-toolkit choices.
- **Evidence:** standardize.py builds the orbit by composing RDKit operations on top of the P_rdkit base; no second real toolkit is used. RQ3_ablation median_abs_delta_by_operation is 0.0 for four of five operations. Section 6.2 attributes the mild aggregate effect partly to providers having pre-standardized the data.
- **Fix:** Add an experiment that standardizes each dataset end-to-end with 2-3 genuinely different real toolkits/workflows (e.g., RDKit vs OpenBabel vs a datamol/ChemAxon-style pipeline) and measures per-prediction disagreement on that realistic set, then report the enumerated-orbit number as a controlled upper-bound companion. This directly tests the between-lab claim the paper makes.
- **Requires new experiment:** True

### [MAJOR] Deduplication and split integrity (research_plan.md Splitting; splits.py scaffold_split; leakage_audit.csv)
- **Issue:** The pre-registration explicitly committed to: 'before every split, standardize to a canonical form for duplicate detection; drop exact duplicates; ... never let two standardization variants of the same molecule cross the split (split on canonical-graph identity).' None of this is implemented. scaffold_split groups by Murcko scaffold of the RAW SMILES and neither drops canonical-identity duplicates nor splits on canonical identity; no dedup step exists anywhere in src/. Consequently 851 conflicting-label duplicate groups remain in the data (e.g., ppbr_az 303 disagreeing groups, solubility 232, clearance_hepatocyte 122, ames/cyp groups), and 82 molecules leak across the scaffold split (solubility 27, ames 18, herg 10, cyp2d6 9) — contradicting leakage_audit.py's own comment that leakage is '0 by construction of our identity-aware splitter'. For RQ1/RQ2 the fixed split holds this constant across pipelines, as argued. But the per-prediction invariance metric (RQ5) and the semi-synthetic shortcut (RQ5b) are per-molecule: conflicting-label duplicates inject label noise that models fit idiosyncratically, confounding the orbit SD attributed to standardization, and a canonical molecule appearing with different labels on both sides of the split for some pipelines contaminates exactly the headline invariance measurement.
- **Evidence:** splits.py has no dedup and no canonical-identity grouping; grep finds no dedup/drop_duplicates in src/. leakage_audit.csv scaffold_id_leak column sums to 82 nonzero entries; dup_label_disagree column sums to ~851 groups. research_plan.md lines 117-123 promised the dedup that was not done; the deviation is not disclosed in the manuscript.
- **Fix:** Deduplicate on canonical identity (aggregating/averaging conflicting labels or dropping ambiguous groups) and split on canonical-graph identity as pre-registered, then re-run the per-prediction invariance (RQ5) and shortcut (RQ5b) analyses; report results with and without dedup and disclose the deviation from pre-registration explicitly.
- **Requires new experiment:** True

### [MODERATE] Generalization claims vs actual coverage (Section 8; metrics.json RQ4/RQ5/RQ5b n)
- **Issue:** Section 8 states the findings and SORB's effect are consistent '(iv) across the 55 datasets', but the method, invariance, and shortcut experiments were run on only 49 datasets (RQ4 reg n=160=40 datasets x 4; RQ5_invariance n_datasets=49; RQ5b_shortcut n_datasets=49), excluding the 6 largest (solubility 9982, ld50 7385, ames 7278, cyp2c9 12092, cyp2d6 13130, cyp3a4 12328). These excluded datasets also carry the heaviest duplicate/collapse burden (e.g., cyp2d6 251 dup groups / 273 collapse pairs; ames 618/611), so the method is validated on precisely the subset least affected by the data-quality issues. The exclusion is not clearly disclosed in Sections 7-8.
- **Evidence:** metrics.json RQ5_invariance and RQ5b_shortcut both report n_datasets=49; RQ4 pooled reg n=160 implies 40 regression datasets vs 42 available. Section 8 text asserts 'across the 55 datasets'.
- **Fix:** State plainly that SORB/invariance/shortcut results cover 49 of 55 (or 54) datasets and why; either extend to the 6 large datasets (CPU cost is modest per the paper's own timing) or justify and label the exclusion, and correct the Section 8 '55 datasets' claim.
- **Requires new experiment:** False

### [MINOR] Chemical/endpoint domain breadth (Section 5; Limitations iv)
- **Issue:** All benchmarks are small (<=13k), drug-like small molecules from three related sources (ChEMBL potency, TDC ADMET, 3 MoleculeNet physicochemical), heavily weighted to ChEMBL potency (30 of 55). There is no evaluation on other chemical spaces where standardization choices are more consequential (macrocycles, peptides, zwitterions, metal-containing, agrochemicals, materials) and, as acknowledged, no pretrained graph/sequence models. The 'representation-agnostic' and broad reproducibility claims are therefore demonstrated only on a narrow, mutually similar slice of chemistry and two classical featurizations.
- **Evidence:** Table 1 composition; Limitation (iv) already concedes the model-family gap but not the chemical-domain narrowness.
- **Fix:** Scope the generalization claims to drug-like small molecules with fingerprint/descriptor featurizations, and add a sentence in Limitations about chemical-space breadth (zwitterions/macrocycles where tautomer/protonation choices dominate) as explicit future work; optionally add one out-of-domain dataset as a spot check.
- **Requires new experiment:** False
