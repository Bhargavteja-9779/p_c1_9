# Review 07: R7-compute (Computational Cost & Deployment)

**Recommendation:** major_revision

**Summary:** The diagnostic contribution (standardization-invariance violation is real, representation-dependent, and hidden by aggregate metrics) is sound and worth publishing. But the proposed remedy SORB is not justified on cost/deployment grounds as presented. By the authors' own numbers SORB delivers no accuracy benefit (test-orbit neutral; train-aug significantly worse, p=5e-8) while costing up to 6x inference and 4x training data, and its sole benefit — reduced per-prediction orbit spread — is obtainable at 1x cost by the obvious alternative the paper never tests: applying the training canonicalization (including tautomer canonicalization) to inference inputs. The computational analysis is also under-reported and partly inconsistent with the actual runs: SORB was evaluated only on 49/55 datasets (the 6 largest excluded for memory, never disclosed), timing claims are unmeasured, and core-count/runtime figures do not match the authoritative run. A practitioner reading this would reasonably choose "canonicalize consistently" over SORB.

## Findings

### [MAJOR] Deployment / missing baseline: the cheap alternative that defeats the method
- **Issue:** The central deployment case for SORB-testorbit is that it makes a prediction '(nearly) independent of the arbitrary standardization of its input' (Sec 7.1). But the obvious, free alternative is never tested: apply the SAME canonicalization used at training (P_rdkit + canonical tautomer) to every inference input. In src/training/invariance.py the 'baseline' config feeds each test molecule's orbit members AS-IS (run_method_cell never re-canonicalizes test inputs), so its measured orbit SD is an artifact of NOT canonicalizing. A deployed model that canonicalizes its inputs maps every orbit member to one form (see canonical_identity() in standardize.py, which collapses tautomer/charge/salt/stereo to a single key) and is therefore EXACTLY invariant at 1x cost, versus SORB's only-approximate invariance at up to 6x inference cost with no accuracy gain. For cross-lab agreement SORB still requires a shared orbit definition and averaging convention, so it does not remove the coordination requirement — it makes the required convention ~6x more expensive. Without this comparison the reader cannot judge whether SORB is ever worth adopting.
- **Evidence:** manuscript Sec 7.1 (line 298-301) 'free reliability improvement'; invariance.py run_method_cell uses Xcanon only for baseline/trainaug accuracy and measures baseline invariance via _pipeline_matrix/_orbit_invariance on un-recanonicalized members; metrics.json RQ4 shows testorbit accuracy median improvement -7.45e-5 (p=0.8), i.e. no accuracy upside to offset the 6x cost; canonical_identity() proves a 1x canonical collapse exists.
- **Fix:** Add a 'canonicalize-at-inference' baseline (apply the full training standardization, including canonical tautomer, to every test input) and report its orbit SD (should be ~0) and accuracy against SORB-testorbit. If canonicalize-at-inference matches SORB on reliability at 1x cost, reframe SORB's niche to the specific case where input standardization cannot be controlled or the canonical form is tool-ambiguous, and state this explicitly in Discussion/Sec 9.
- **Requires new experiment:** True

### [MAJOR] Honest overhead reporting: 'free' conflates no-accuracy-cost with no-compute-cost
- **Issue:** Sec 7.1 calls test-time orbit averaging 'a free reliability improvement' and Sec 9 calls it 'the cost-effective default', yet Sec 9 itself states it 'multiplies inference cost by the orbit size (at most 6)'. 'Free' is accurate only for accuracy, not for compute: 6x inference latency, throughput, and energy are first-order deployment concerns (batch scoring of large libraries, latency-bound services). No inference-latency, throughput, or energy numbers are given anywhere; there is no cost-benefit quantification backing 'cost-effective'.
- **Evidence:** manuscript line 298 'free reliability improvement'; lines 344-348 Sec 9 admits '6x' inference but provides no measured latency/throughput; no cost column in any table (tables/table3_method.csv contains only accuracy/invariance).
- **Fix:** Replace 'free' with 'accuracy-neutral at up to 6x inference cost'. Report measured inference latency/throughput for baseline vs SORB-testorbit on a representative dataset, and state when the 6x is and is not acceptable (e.g., single-compound decision support vs million-compound virtual screening).
- **Requires new experiment:** False

### [MAJOR] Scope non-disclosure: SORB untested on the largest datasets, where cost and the problem are worst
- **Issue:** SORB (method + shortcut) ran only on the 49 datasets with <=6000 molecules; the 6 largest (up to cyp2d6_veith, 13,130 mol) were excluded because, per the run_all.py code comment, 'orbit augmentation on very large sets is memory-heavy'. The manuscript never states this: the strings '49' and '6000' do not appear in manuscript_filled.md, and Sec 8 claims the method-level conclusions hold '(iv) across the 55 datasets'. The excluded CYP-inhibition sets are exactly the large, highly-ionizable/tautomerizable datasets where RQ3 predicts the invariance violation is worst AND where 6x inference cost hurts most — so the method's deployability is unvalidated precisely where it matters, and the overhead/scalability limitation is hidden.
- **Evidence:** run_all.py lines 183-191 'cap to datasets <= METHOD_MAX_N ... memory-heavy', METHOD_MAX_N=6000; metrics.json RQ5b n_datasets=49; full_run5.log '[E4/E5 method ...] on 49/55 datasets (<= 6000 mol)'; table1_datasets.csv max N=13,130 with 6 datasets >6000; manuscript Sec 8 line 336 '(iv) across the 55 datasets'; grep shows no '49'/'6000' in manuscript.
- **Fix:** State clearly that SORB was evaluated on 49/55 datasets (<=6000 molecules) and why (memory), and correct Sec 8 to not claim 55. Add this as an explicit Limitation. To actually support deployment claims, run SORB (at least test-orbit averaging, which has no training-memory issue) on the 6 large datasets, or demonstrate a streaming/memory-bounded augmentation implementation.
- **Requires new experiment:** True

### [MODERATE] Unsupported timing claim for orbit construction
- **Issue:** Sec 9 states 'building orbits for the largest dataset (13,130 molecules) takes under a minute on one core.' There is no timing instrumentation that isolates orbit construction anywhere in src/ (grep for perf_counter/time in src returns nothing; the only time.time() calls in run_all.py report cumulative per-20-job wall time mixed with featurization, inside a parallel Pool, not single-core orbit-only time). The authoritative run that produced metrics.json (full_run5.log) skipped precompute entirely (no '[precompute caches]' line), so no orbit was built in that run, and the 13,130-molecule dataset's orbit is never even used by SORB (it is one of the 6 excluded). The claim is an unverified assertion about a cost the method never actually pays.
- **Evidence:** manuscript lines 343-345; grep 'perf_counter|elapsed|timeit' in src/ returns nothing; cache.py dataset_orbits has no timing; full_run5.log begins at '[E1/E2 sensitivity grid]' with no precompute stage; dataset excluded per METHOD_MAX_N=6000.
- **Fix:** Either measure orbit-construction time explicitly (single core, isolated, reported with RDKit/Dimorphite-DL versions) on representative datasets and report the number with its provenance, or remove the specific claim. Do not cite the 13,130-molecule dataset's orbit time when that orbit is not used by the method.
- **Requires new experiment:** True

### [MODERATE] Runtime/hardware figures inconsistent with the actual authoritative run
- **Issue:** Sec 9 says 'All experiments run on four CPU cores ... the complete study completes in a few hours.' The authoritative run that wrote metrics.json (full_run5.log, timestamped with metrics.json) used nproc=2, and results were in fact accreted across five separate cached runs using 1, 2, 3, and 4 cores. Because caching makes every sensitivity/ablation/method step report '(0s)' in that run, no single cold end-to-end timing of 'a few hours' was ever actually produced; it is an estimate (run_all.py docstring says '~2 h on 4 cores') presented as a measured fact.
- **Evidence:** grep of logs: full_run.log nproc=4, full_run2 nproc=2, full_run3 nproc=3, full_run4 nproc=1, full_run5 nproc=2; full_run5.log shows every sens/abl/method step '(0s)' (cached), only shortcut takes 558s; run_all.py line 157 default nproc and docstring '~2 h wall on 4 cores'.
- **Fix:** Report the actual hardware/core count used for the authoritative run and give a measured cold (cache-cleared) end-to-end wall time, or clearly label the runtime as an estimate. Reconcile '4 cores' with the 2-core authoritative run.
- **Requires new experiment:** False

### [MODERATE] Cost-benefit of train-time augmentation not honestly weighed
- **Issue:** Train-aug costs 4x training data (train_aug=4 in run_method_cell / SORB) yet significantly WORSENS regression accuracy (pooled median improvement -0.013 RMSE, only 32% of cases improved, Wilcoxon p=5.1e-8; for linear+ECFP regression mean -3.29 RMSE, catastrophic). Its only benefit is a 36% reduction in orbit SD — which, as in Finding 1, is obtainable at 1x via consistent canonicalization. The paper recommends train-aug 'specifically for robust (tree-ensemble) learners' where it is ~neutral, but neutral accuracy at 4x training cost with a reliability gain that a free alternative already provides is not a deployment win, and this trade is never framed as a cost-benefit decision.
- **Evidence:** metrics.json RQ4 reg_sorb_trainaug median -0.013, frac 0.319, p=5.14e-08; statistics.json linear/ecfp/reg trainaug mean_improvement -3.29 RMSE; trainaug_invariance 36.3% reduction; SORB default train_aug=4 (sorb.py line 35, invariance.py line 70).
- **Fix:** Present train-aug as a cost (4x data) vs benefit (invariance reduction only, no accuracy gain, possible accuracy loss) decision, and justify the 'adopt for trees' recommendation against the free canonicalize-at-inference alternative rather than only against the as-is baseline.
- **Requires new experiment:** False

### [MODERATE] 'Accuracy-neutral' claim rests on mismatched test sets
- **Issue:** The headline that test-orbit averaging is 'accuracy-neutral' (justifying its cost by reliability alone) compares baseline accuracy computed on the FULL test set against SORB-testorbit/full accuracy computed on a <=600-molecule random subsample (ACC_CAP=600). For the 6 method datasets with n_test>600 (max 840) the paired comparison is on different test subsets, confounding the very 'no accuracy cost' conclusion that the compute justification depends on.
- **Evidence:** invariance.py lines 24, 80-81, 97, 104-108: baseline uses predict_scores(..., Xcanon[te]) on full te; sorb_testorbit/sorb_full use est.predict(cap_smi, ...) with cap capped at ACC_CAP=600; method_results.csv: 6/49 datasets have n_test>600 (max 840).
- **Fix:** Compute all variants' accuracy on the identical (capped) test subset, or raise/remove the cap, and re-report the testorbit accuracy-neutrality (Wilcoxon over datasets) on matched samples.
- **Requires new experiment:** False
